And, the reason why even you do a different amount of damage on different casts of the same spell is because of crit. chance. Sometimes you land a critical hit, so your damage gets multiplied, by whatever your crit multiplier is.

What to Buff

Besides the satisfaction of feeling like a nerd, what we come away with by understanding the above is that there are four things we need to boost, if we want to have sexy damage numbers:

  1. Our max stamina or magicka
  2. Our max weapon or spell damage
  3. Our weapon or spell crit. chance
  4. Our crit. multiplier

When we set up a character, we basically try to make all four as high as we can.

  1. We put all our attribute points into either stamina or magicka.
  2. We make all our jewelery glyphs either weapon or spell damage.
  3. We use the theif mundus and all divines traits on our clothing.
  4. We wear as much medium armor as possible
    (usually 5-7 for nightblades, 4-6 for necromancers, and 3-5 for everyone else)

This gives us high “base stats” in the four important categories, but when we are actually playing the character, we want to “buff” those four even higher.

How to do Buff #1- Max Resource

We buff our max resource with food.

Single-stat “green” food gives the strongest buff. But often it’s preferable to sacrifice a little bit of this buff for some other bonus.

Bi-stat “blue” food will give a little less buff to our max resource, but a lot more buff to some secondary thing (health or recovery). This is what most people use most of the time.

Tri-stat “purple” food gives still less buff to our max resource but gives it to all three.

Special “gold” food is hella expensive and only slightly better than regular blues or purples.

How to do Buff #2 – Weapon / Spell Damage

A buff called “Sorcery” increases our spell damage, and a different buff called “Brutality” increases our weapon damage.

Each one has a “major” and a “minor” version. The major version increases by 20%. The minor version, by 10%.

We get the minor version by having certain classes in our group, or by using certain poisons. The poison, however, is a bad idea, because we would have to sacrifice the damage from our weapon glyph.

We get the major version from potions or from using certain spells.

How to do Buff #3 – Critical Chance

A buff called “Prophecy” increases our spell critical chance, and a different one called “Savagery” increases our weapon critical chance. Again, both have a “major” and a “minor” version. And we get them the same way we get Sorcery and Brutality – from classes (or poisons) and potions (or skills)

How to do Buff #4 – Critical Multiplier

This buff is called “Force” and, you guessed it, there is a “major” and a “minor” version. The most common way to get Minor Force is by casting a certain spell (trap, or accelerate, for example). The most common way to get Major force is by having someone blow a Warhorn.

How to Compose a Dungeon Group

Buff #2 and #3 – to DMG and Crit Chance – are the most complicated, especially since ESO “hybridized” their damage calculations.

With 12-player trial groups, it’s easier for these two buffs to just happen by mistake, but definitely, in 4-player dungeon groups, we have to be really careful about them.

Stacking the DMG Buff (#2)

Templars give Minor Sorcery, and Dragonknights give Minor Brutality, so…

Now we have to stack our Major buff with whatever Minor buff we got above. So…

But here is where it gets messy!!! What if my max stat is Magicka, and there is a Dragonknight and no Templar in my group? Then, I would use weapon glyphs and yellow Weapon Power potions – but I will lose the part of the potion that helps me sustain my magicka! =(

The easiest and best way to get Major Brutality (or Sorcery) is to ask one Dragonknight to use Igneous Weapons. (It’s “easiest” because only one person needs to use it, and it gives the buff to the whole group for a whole minute.) But there are other ways that might be better sometimes, in 4-player groups. Here are some options. Remember these don’t buff the whole group, they only buff the caster).

  • Anyone can cast
    degeneration (mages guild),
    any momentum morph (two-handed), or
    hidden blade (dual-wield)
  • Nightblades can cast any sap essence morph
  • Sorcerers can cast any surge morph
  • Wardens can cast any netch morph

Stacking the Crit Chance Buff (#3)

Once our DMG buff is stacked right, we want to plan ahead to make sure our Crit Chance buff will also stack. Sorcerers give Minor Prophecy to their group, and Nightblades give Minor Savagery. So…

But again it’s messy if our max resource isn’t benefitted by the type of potion we need. In those cases, we can try to get the Major Crit buff ourselves, from something else. Here is how to get either version of the Major Crit buff.

  • Anyone can slot
    any Hunter morph (figher’s guild), or
    any Magelight morph (mage’s guild)
  • Templars can cast any Sun Fire morph
  • Dragonknights can slot any Inferno morph
  • Wardens can cast any Lotus Flower morph

A Last Word on “Hybridization”

We do not have to match our crit stat to our dmg stat. For example, if weapon damage is my highest damage stat and spell crit is my highest crit chance, that is not a problem at all.

So, if I have weapon dmg glyphs, I do need to make sure I get major and minor Brutality (not Sorcerer), but I am not locked in to getting the Savagery buff. I can get either Savagery or Prophecy – I just have to make sure I get the Major and Minor version of either one.

The only thing really making hybridization a complicated mess right now, is that ESO still has not hybridized potion effects or added new potion ingredients to let us make more “hybrid” concoctions.
